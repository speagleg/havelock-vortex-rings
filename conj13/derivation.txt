Montaldi-Tokieda Conjecture 13: stability of the regular n-gon AT its bifurcation point
=====================================================================================================
2026-10-10. Status: DRAFT, no second reader yet. Labels follow VERIFICATION_POLICY.md.

0. Source and exact statement
-----------------------------
J. Montaldi, T. Tokieda, "Deformation of geometry and bifurcations of vortex rings", arXiv:1210.5662v1
(Recent Trends in Dynamical Systems, Springer PROMS 35 (2013) 335-370). The arXiv PDF was read in full
(pdftotext of a local copy; not committed).

Setting (MT Sec. 1-2): one smooth family of surfaces M_lambda with curvature 4*lambda, uniform chart C,
symplectic form 2dx^dy/sigma^2 with sigma = 1+lambda|z|^2, and the Green's function (1.12)
log(|z-w|^2/(sigma_z sigma_w)), i.e. "the family defined in (1.12) with the constant background
vorticity". lambda<0 is the hyperbolic plane with the SINH model of this item (note.tex); lambda=0 is the
plane; lambda>0 is the standard sphere model. Parameter lambda*r0^2 in (-1,1].

MT p.15, Theorem 6 preamble (verbatim): "Stability means Lyapunov stability modulo rotations (same as
orbital stability in our situation). Instability means full spectral instability, i.e. at least one of the
eigenvalues is real and positive. The ring of n = 3 vortices is always nonlinearly stable."
Theorem 6, "all n": "The ring is stable for lambda r0^2 < b_n, where b_n is the unique root in (-1,1] of
(1+b_n^2)/(1+b_n)^2 = (1/(2(n-1))) floor(n^2/4)."   (3.6)
Corollary 7 (verbatim): "The Thomson heptagon is nonlinearly stable."
  (introduced as: "As a special case, we recover the following result of Kurakin and Yudovich [11] and
  Schmidt [20]. The calculation justifying it is the subject of section 3.5.2.")
MT p.23 (verbatim):
  "Conjecture 13. For all values of n, the n-gon is stable at the bifurcation point.
   Above, we have proved this for all even n and for n = 3, 5, 7, 9, 11."

So the conjecture concerns lambda*r0^2 = b_n: sphere for n = 4, 5, 6 (b_n = 0.268, 0.172, 0.0557), plane
for n = 7 (b_7 = 0), hyperbolic plane (sinh model) for n >= 8 (b_n = -xi*(n) of note.tex; b_8 = -(8-3sqrt7)).
It is NOT a statement about the tanh (image/Kimura) model; Sec. 5 below treats that model separately.

Relevant MT machinery (verbatim excerpts, p.19-23):
 Lemma 8: f analytic near 0, degenerate critical point, B = d2f(0), C = d3f(0), D = d4f(0). "If B is
   positive-semidefinite and if for all a in Ker B\{0}, b in R^n we have Ca^3 = 0,
   Da^4 + 6Ca^2b + 3Bb^2 > 0, then f is strictly positive on a punctured neighbourhood of 0."
 Proposition 9: "If the hypotheses of Lemma 8 are satisfied with R^n replaced by N and f by H^, then the
   relative equilibrium is Lyapunov stable." (N = symplectic slice, a G_x-invariant complement to
   T0 = g.x cap Ker dJ inside Ker dJ; H^ = H - omega J the augmented Hamiltonian; the proof says the
   hypotheses "applied on M_mu are equivalent to the same hypotheses applied on N".)
 Lemma 10 (even n): "For all a in V_{n/2} and b in N, we have Ca^2b = 0."
 (3.9) (odd n): "The key quantity Da4 + Ca2b + Bb2 becomes, on completing the square,
   delta|z|^4 + 2gamma Re(z^2 w) + beta|w|^2 = ... Manifestly this is positive ... iff delta > 0 and
   beta delta > gamma^2."  n = 7: beta = 21/(2 pi r0^2), gamma = 63/(4 pi r0^3), delta = 1071/(8 pi r0^4).

2. Method
---------
Reduced space M_mu = J^{-1}(mu)/G_mu near the ring; G_mu = SO(2) (rotations about the ring centre) for
every lambda; the full group is SO(3)/SE(2)/SL(2,R). Energy-Casimir/energy-momentum argument: if the
reduced Hamiltonian has a strict local minimum at the ring, the ring is Lyapunov stable modulo G_mu (this
step is MT Prop. 9, which relies on their reference [17]; I have not read [17]). At b_n the Hessian of the
reduced Hamiltonian is positive semidefinite with kernel = critical mode (radial component of Fourier mode
l = floor(n/2); dim 1 for even n, 2 for odd n). All other directions are strictly positive: the Fourier
eigenvalues (MT (2.6); note.tex Prop. 2) are a_m = T - m(n-m)/2 > 0 for m != floor(n/2), ceil(n/2), b_m > 0, and
eps'_1 > 0 on V1'. Numerically (engine.py, 40 digits) the smallest non-kernel eigenvalue of the
constrained Hessian is 1.0 (even n) or 2.0 (odd n) for every case below, and 14.756 for tanh n = 8.

Decisive quantity: the effective quartic c4 of the reduced Hamiltonian along the kernel, after
eliminating ("slaving") all other directions, ON THE LEVEL SET J = mu:
   f_eff(eps) = min{ H(q) - H(q0) : q = q0 + eps e + v, v _|_ e, v _|_ t, J(q) = J(q0) } = c4 eps^4 + O(eps^6)
(e = unit Cartesian kernel vector in the uniform chart C; normalisation r0 = 1, lambda = b_n, H without
the 1/(4 pi); signs are normalisation-free). The cubic term vanishes by symmetry, for even n because
the rotation c acts as -1 on V_{n/2}, for odd n by MT Prop. 12(i). Numerically |c3| < 1e-24 everywhere.

Symmetry (even n, the clean case). V_{n/2} carries the D_2 action with c = -1 on the mode, so f_eff is even.
Since a (x) a for a in V_{n/2} lies in Fourier mode 0, the only directions that couple to a^2 at cubic order
are mode 0. Its tangential part is the rotation (gauge) and its radial part is fixed by J3 = mu. The other
momentum components (mode 1) get no a^2 correction. Better still, a is fixed by D_{n/2} = <c^2, m>. The
minimiser of the strictly convex slaved problem is unique, so it is D_{n/2}-invariant (symmetric
criticality). Fix(D_{n/2}) is exactly the 2-parameter family of two staggered regular n/2-gons with radii
rho_+, rho_- (MT Sec. 3.4). So f_eff is EXACTLY (to all orders) the restriction of H to the curve
{J3 = mu} in this 2-parameter family. With M = n/2, u = rho^2 and the product formulas
   prod_{zeta^M=-1} (x - y zeta) = x^M + y^M,   prod_{k=1}^{M-1} (1 - u eta^k) = (1-u^M)/(1-u),
the pair sum is   sum log|z_i-z_j|^2 = M(M-1)/2 (log u+ + log u-) + 2M log(u+^{M/2} + u-^{M/2}) + const,
and on the curve g(u_pm) = g0 +- tau, g(u) = u/(1+lambda u), everything is elementary (even_exact.py).

3. Two problems in MT's proof (found here; conclusions survive)
-----------------------------------------------------------------
(P1) Linear slice vs level set. Lemma 8 is applied to H^ restricted to the LINEAR slice q0 + N
  (Prop. 9; in the even case, MT expand along the straight line z_j = (r0 + (-1)^j t) e^{2 pi i j/n}, which
  changes J3 at O(t^2)). Because the kernel is degenerate, the 4th-order term of the reduced Hamiltonian
  also contains the curvature of J^{-1}(mu): the O(eps^2) correction along the radial mode-0 direction,
  which Lemma 10 / Prop. 12(iii) exclude because "V0 cap N = {0}". These terms are not zero:
    n=8 : level set c4 = 95/56 = 1.6964...,  linear slice 2.27794...
    n=7 : level set 9/14,                    linear slice 6/7.
  The linear-slice quantity is not the reduced Hamiltonian's quartic coefficient, so Prop. 9 as stated
  does not apply. The coefficient that does decide stability is the level-set c4.
(P2) Weights in (3.9). With Lemma 8's weights the completed square is delta - 3 gamma^2/beta (the display
  in (3.9) shows delta - gamma^2/beta; we read the weights as intended). Check: with MT's n=7 values, (delta - 3gamma^2/beta)/delta = 8/17, which is exactly
  the ratio (linear-slice min)/(straight line) = (6/7)/(51/28) computed independently by engine.py
  (mt_n7_check.out). MT's printed ratio is 14/17. MT's n = 5, 9, 11 numbers stay positive with the weights
  (beta delta - 3gamma^2 = 341, 687, 4015 in their units).
(P3, minor) MT's printed even-n t^4 coefficient T differs slightly from our symbolic recomputation of the same
  straight-line expansion. The relative difference is 9b^2(...)/(...), i.e. 0.1% at n=8 and 0.5% at n=10.
  Our version agrees with engine.py to 15 digits. It does not affect any sign.

4. Results: MT family (the conjecture's own setting)
----------------------------------------------------
4a. Even n: exact, all n (PROVED modulo MT Prop. 9's reduction step and the second-reader check).
  even_exact.py (sympy, symbolic n = 2M and b):
    h(tau) = h2 tau^2 + h4 tau^4 + ...,  h2 = -(M/4)(1+b)^2 [M^2(1+b)^2 - 4M(1+b^2) + 2(1+b^2)].
  h2 vanishes exactly on MT's threshold (3.7) (polynomial remainder 0). On the threshold
  (even_formula.py, remainder 0):
    h4 = (n-1)(n-2)(1+b)^4(1+b^2)^2 (m^4 + 40m^2 + 48m + 16) / (48 n^3),   m = n-2,
  which is manifestly > 0 for every even n >= 4. In the unit-kernel-vector normalisation:
    c4_even(n) = (n-2)(n^4 - 8n^3 + 64n^2 - 144n + 96) / (192 n (n-1))
               = 1/4, 23/30, 95/56, 49/15, 755/132 for n = 4, 6, 8, 10, 12.
  engine.py (full 2n-dimensional constrained minimisation, no symmetry assumed) reproduces these to 15
  digits (out/rq_MT_*.out). This independently confirms the symmetric-criticality reduction.
  n = 4, 6 are the sphere thresholds b = 2-sqrt3, 9-4sqrt5; n = 8 is the sinh threshold
  b = -(8-3sqrt7), where c4 = 95/56.
4b. Odd n: NUMERICAL (40-digit constrained minimisation, eps-fit with c2, c3 ~ 1e-25).
  n    b_n            level-set c4 (true)   linear-slice (MT P1)
  5    +0.171573      0.225 = 9/40          0.204657   (sphere)

5. tanh (image / "Kimura") model on H^2: not part of Conjecture 13
------------------------------------------------------------------
Even n only, because for odd n the critical block has g_m != 0 and the energy-momentum test fails at the
spectral threshold (tanh/derivation.txt Sec. 4). The same Fix(D_{n/2}) reduction applies. The extra image
term is again a product: sum log|1 - z_i conj z_j|^2 = M log[(1-u+^M)/(1-u+)] + M log[(1-u-^M)/(1-u-)]
+ 2M log(1 + (u+u-)^{M/2}). The momentum is J3 = sum u/(1-u). tanh_even_exact.py:
 - INDEPENDENT CHECK OF THE UNREVIEWED tanh THRESHOLD: h2 has numerator
   -2(u-1)^4 (u^10 - 12u^9 - 37u^8 - 72u^7 - 84u^6 - 88u^5 - 84u^4 - 72u^3 - 37u^2 - 12u + 1), exactly the
   minimal polynomial in tanh/derivation.txt. Its root in (0,1) is
   xi*(8) = 0.0673562230040812622684374765662355743940251175697... (exact rational isolating interval of
   width 1e-141). n = 10, 12 likewise reproduce the derivation's xi* to 50 digits.
   This checks the mode-N/2 threshold equation, not the claim that N/2 is the critical mode.
 - h4(u0) for n = 8 is (u0-1)^6 * [degree-22 palindromic polynomial with ALL coefficients positive] /
   (positive denominator). So h4 > 0 on all of (0,1), in particular at xi*(8). Arb ball:
   h4(xi*) = 188389.6854000151821696240652 +- 4e-25. Also n = 10: 6152.61... and n = 12: 1346.96..., both > 0.
 - engine.py (tanh, n = 8): level-set c4 = 373.2727, linear slice 502.43; smallest non-kernel constrained
   eigenvalue 14.756 (NUMERICAL).
 Status: the sign of the quartic is exact. Transverse positivity at xi* is NUMERICAL. The tanh threshold
 itself is still unreviewed (but now confirmed by a second route).

6. Direct integrations (integrate.py; NUMERICAL; independent code)
-----------------------------------------------------------------
Disk model, area form 4dx^dy/(1-|z|^2)^2, DOP853 with rtol 1e-12, T = 2000 (about 6+ periods of the
amplitude-1e-4 nonlinear oscillation at threshold). Initial data: ring at xi*(1+rel) plus the critical mode
|z_k| = sqrt(xi)(1 + delta(-1)^k). Diagnostic: D(t) = max_{i<j} |d_ij(t) - d_ij(ring)| in hyperbolic
distances, which is invariant under the full group. Conservation: |dH| < 1e-8, |dJ| < 6e-10. The
unperturbed ring drifts by < 6e-12.
  max_t D/D(0)        rel=-1e-2   -1e-3    0       +1e-3   +1e-2
  sinh n=8, d=1e-3     29.34       9.13    1.000   1.000   1.000
  sinh n=8, d=1e-4    293.6       91.86    1.000   1.000   1.000
  tanh n=8, d=1e-3     28.38       8.82    1.000   1.000   1.000
  tanh n=8, d=1e-4    284.0       88.85    1.000   1.000   1.000
On the unstable side the deviation saturates at an amplitude independent of delta (about 0.029 for
rel = -1e-2 and 0.0091 for rel = -1e-3, ratio about sqrt10). That is the supercritical-pitchfork
signature: the orbit sits near the stable bifurcated pair of n/2-gons. At threshold and on the stable side
D(t) never exceeds D(0) and keeps oscillating, spending 38-42% of the time below D(0)/2
(out/integration_summary.out), with no secular growth over the four quarters. With a negative quartic,
the threshold runs would leave the delta-neighbourhood on the nonlinear time scale. This is consistent
with stability at threshold, but it is not a proof.

7. Verdict
----------
- Even n (MT family: sphere n = 4, 6 and sinh-H^2 n >= 8, including n = 8 at xi* = 8-3sqrt7): Conjecture 13
  holds. The correct reduced quartic is given by an exact closed form, positive for all even n. This
  repairs MT's even-n proof, whose conclusion was right but whose slice argument (P1) was incomplete.
  Label: PROVED (exact symbolic) modulo (i) MT Prop. 9 / ref [17] (strict min of the reduced Hamiltonian
  implies Lyapunov stability modulo G_mu), and (ii) second-reader review of the symmetric-criticality step.
- Odd n: NUMERICAL. c4 > 0 for n = 5, ..., 25, with the identified formula (n-1)(n-3)(n^2-9n+38)/(128n)
  verified out of sample. For odd n >= 13 this is new evidence for the open part. It is not a proof for
  all n.
- tanh model, n = 8 (and 10, 12): the even-n analogue holds (exact sign of the quartic, numerical
  transverse positivity).
- Novelty: POSSIBLY-NEW (the level-set correction, the even-n closed form, the odd-n formula, the tanh
  analogue). KNOWN: the even-n conclusion (claimed by MT) and n = 7 (KY 2002). Sphere n = 4, 5, 6: possibly in
  Kurakin 2004 (unread).

8. What a second reader must check
----------------------------------
1. That the level-set f_eff (not MT's linear slice) is the right object, i.e. (P1). Concretely, check that
   the O(eps^2) correction along the radial mode-0 direction changes the eps^4 coefficient. (n = 8: 95/56 vs
   2.2779; the closed forms make this checkable by hand.)
2. The symmetric-criticality step: Fix(D_{n/2}) cap J^{-1}(mu) is the whole slaving manifold, and the
   mode-1 momenta get no a^2 term.
3. The product formulas and the closed-form pair sums (numerically checked in both scripts).
4. MT Prop. 9's use of [17] for noncompact G (SL(2,R)) with G_mu = SO(2): its exact hypotheses.
5. The factor-3 claim (P2) against Lemma 8's weights.
6. Odd n: a proof of c4_odd(n). Route: write s_k = eps cos(l_c phi_k) + eps^2(modes 0, 1) and
   theta_k = eps^2(mode 1), expand log(2cosh ds - 2cos(phi + dtheta)) to 4th order, and reduce the sums
   to S_s(p) = sum_d e^{i p phi_d} (1 - cos phi_d)^{-s}, s <= 3. These are polynomials in (n, p) for
   0 <= p <= n, provable by the second-difference recursion in p.
7. Kurakin 2004 (sphere) and Kurakin-Yudovich 2002 (63/16): read them.
