\begin{abstract}
Montaldi and Tokieda~\cite[\S4.2]{MT} considered the image Green's function on the hyperbolic plane, whose pair
interaction is $-\ln\tanh(d/2)$ (the Hamiltonian they attribute to Kimura), and wrote that ``the stability problem
seems no longer tractable analytically''. Boatto~\cite{Boatto} had earlier computed the stability ranges of the regular
$N$-ring in this model numerically. We show that the linear problem is tractable. In log-polar coordinates the Hessian
of the augmented Hamiltonian at the ring splits into $2\times2$ Hermitian Fourier blocks whose entries are explicit
rational functions of $\xi=\tanh^2(r/2)$, where $r$ is the hyperbolic ring radius. Unlike the ``sinh'' model, the blocks
have a radial--tangential coupling. Under $\xi\mapsto1/\xi$ the diagonal entries are invariant and the coupling
changes sign; this reciprocity comes from inversion in the boundary circle. It makes the threshold polynomials
palindromic. For $N=8,\dots,16$ we certify the spectral threshold $\xi_*(N)$ (exact rational root isolation and Arb
ball arithmetic) and the critical mode $\lfloor N/2\rfloor$. We also certify, with PARI/GP and an independent
mod-$p$ factorisation test, that $\xi_*(N)$ has an irreducible palindromic minimal polynomial of degree $2N-2$,
$\frac32N-2$ or $\frac32N-3$ according to $N$ odd, $N\equiv0$ or $N\equiv2\pmod 4$. The number $\xi_*(8)$ is an algebraic
unit of degree $10$; for $9\le N\le16$, $\xi_*(N)$ is not an algebraic integer. (That the thresholds are algebraic is
automatic once the blocks are rational; the content is their explicit structure.) Rings with $N\le7$ are stable at
every radius. For odd $N$ the energy--momentum (Hessian-definiteness) threshold lies strictly above the spectral
threshold. In the gap the ring is linearly (spectrally) stable, and its nonlinear stability is undecided (a Krein
window). In the variable $\tau=-\frac N2\ln\xi$ the blocks are, up to an $m$-independent term of order $N^{-2}$, a
function of $m/N$. From this exact identity we prove, for every $N$ and every radius, that $b_m>0$ and that the critical
mode is $\lfloor N/2\rfloor$; for every even $N\ge8$ that the threshold is unique and equals the energy--momentum
threshold; and for every odd $N\ge9$ that the Krein window is non-empty, of width $O(N^{-3})$. We also prove
$\cosh r_*=\kappa_\infty N+O(1/N)$, with $\kappa_\infty=1/(2\eta_\infty)\approx0.1343122$ and $\eta_\infty$ the root of
$(\coth\eta-1/\eta)^2=2\tanh(\eta)/\eta$. Physically, a large ring stabilises once neighbouring vortices are more than
$\ell_*=\pi/\eta_\infty\approx0.844$ curvature radii apart, which is exactly the critical spacing of an infinite vortex row
on a horocycle. In
the sinh model, $\cosh r_*\sim\sqrt N/2$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Motivation and prior work}\label{sec:motivation}
\paragraph{The question left open by Montaldi--Tokieda.} In \S4.2 of~\cite{MT} (arXiv:1210.5662v1, p.~25), for the
Green's function $G=\log\frac{|z-w|^2}{|1+\lambda z\bar w|^2}$, Montaldi and Tokieda write: ``On the hyperbolic plane
$\lambda<0$ this reduces to the Hamiltonian adopted by Kimura [10].'' After discussing $\lambda>0$, they write:
\begin{quote}
``The calculations based on this option of Green's function get so cumbersome that the stability problem seems no
longer tractable analytically. It seems likely that the results are similar to those in section 3, though the details
of where the bifurcations occur will differ.''
\end{quote}
At $\lambda=-1$ this Green's function is $\log\tanh^2(d/2)$, i.e.\ the model (\ref{H}) below. Our results answer the
first sentence, for linear stability. The Hessian at the ring reduces to explicit rational $2\times2$ blocks
(Thm.~\ref{thm:blocks}). The threshold is the root of an explicit polynomial equation (\S\ref{sec:recip}), and for
$N\le16$ it is certified together with its minimal polynomial (\S\ref{sec:cert}). On the guess in the second sentence:
the stability picture of their \S2 carries over qualitatively. Rings with $N\le7$ are stable at every radius; for
$N\ge8$ a ring is stable exactly beyond a threshold radius, and the critical mode is $\lfloor N/2\rfloor$ (proved for
every $N$ for the critical mode, and for every even $N$ for the uniqueness of the threshold; \S\ref{sec:asym}). There is
one structural difference. The tanh blocks have a radial--tangential coupling, and for odd $N$ it separates the spectral
threshold from the energy--momentum threshold (\S\ref{sec:krein}); this has no counterpart in the sinh model. We do not
study the bifurcations themselves, which are the subject of their \S3. So their guess about the bifurcation types
remains open.

\paragraph{Prior work on this model.} The record of what we read is in \texttt{literature.md}.
\begin{itemize}
\item Boatto~\cite{Boatto} studies exactly this model: on the hyperbolic surface her Green's function is
``$\Psi_K=-\frac1{2\pi}\ln\tanh(\frac{\sqrt{|K|}}2r)$''. She writes the Hessian in the rotating frame in block form with
``$\mathcal Q$ and $\mathcal P$ \dots\ symmetric circulant matrices, while $\mathcal G$ is an asymmetric circulant
matrix'', i.e.\ the Fourier-block structure with a coupling term. She then ``(numerically) monitor[s] the maximum and
the minimum eigenvalues'' and concludes that ``for $N\ge7$, we can only assure stability for sufficiently negative
$K$'' (her Fig.~5a, $N=5,\dots,9$, ring of geodesic radius $1$). So the stability question, the block structure and the
qualitative picture for $N\ge8$ are hers. We did not find in her paper closed forms for the block entries,
threshold values beyond plotting accuracy, the algebraic structure of the thresholds, or the odd-$N$ separation of the
two thresholds. Her criterion is Hessian definiteness, i.e.\ our energy--momentum threshold. Her ring has fixed
geodesic radius $1$ while $K$ varies, so $K=-r^2$ with $\xi=\tanh^2(r/2)$. Implemented as written, her equations
(14), (17) and (31) reproduce our thresholds exactly: $K_*(8)=-0.28218454857650$, i.e.\ $\xi_*(8)$ to $10^{-41}$, and
$K_{\rm EM}(9)=-0.48869837$, i.e.\ our $\xi_{\rm EM}(9)$, including the certified gap $\xi_{\rm EM}-\xi_*=3.22\cdot10^{-8}$.
Her Fourier blocks equal ours up to $m$-independent factors. The zero crossings in her Fig.~5a lie slightly elsewhere, at
$K\approx-0.2465$ ($N=8$) and $-0.3964$ ($N=9$), digitised to $\pm0.002$. These are reproduced to plotting accuracy by the pair
law $-\ln\sinh D$ ($D$ the full geodesic distance), i.e.\ her eq.~(17) with the sign of the $\ln(d+1)$ term flipped,
which gives $-0.24656$ and $-0.39692$. The difference therefore seems to lie in the computation behind the plotted
$K<0$ curves rather than in the model, and it leaves her qualitative conclusions unchanged; her $K>0$ curves agree
with her equations. We may of course have misread her conventions. Details and scripts: \texttt{tanh/boatto\_check/}.
\item Montaldi--Nava-Gaxiola~\cite{MNG} and Nava-Gaxiola's thesis~\cite{NG} use the same Hamiltonian but analyse only
$N=2,3$. Both point to Hwang--Kim~\cite{HK} for rings.
\item Hwang--Kim 2013~\cite{HK}: we saw only the abstract (``the configuration of rings of vortices is investigated
in detail''). According to~\cite{NG} it uses the same Hamiltonian and gives conditions for ring relative equilibria.
Whether it contains stability thresholds is unknown.
\item Kimura~\cite{Kimura}: abstract only.
\end{itemize}
Hence the closed-form blocks, the reciprocity, the certified table, the minimal polynomials and the odd-$N$ window
are, as far as we can tell, new, subject to Hwang--Kim 2013, which we have not read.

\section{The model}
\subsection{Hamiltonian}
We use the Poincar\'e disk $\D=\{|z|<1\}$ with the metric $4|dz|^2/(1-|z|^2)^2$ and the area (symplectic) form
$\omega=\sum_k\lambda(z_k)\,dx_k\wedge dy_k$, $\lambda(z)=4/(1-|z|^2)^2$. The geodesic distance satisfies
$\tanh(d(z,w)/2)=|z-w|/|1-z\bar w|$. For $N$ unit vortices, take
\begin{equation}\label{H}
H=-\sum_{j<k}\ln\tanh\frac{d_{jk}}2=\sum_{j<k}\Big(-\ln|z_j-z_k|+\ln|1-z_j\bar z_k|\Big),
\qquad J=\sum_k\frac{1+|z_k|^2}{1-|z_k|^2}=\sum_k\cosh r_k .
\end{equation}
Here $J$ generates rotations about $0$, and Hamilton's equations are $\dot x_k=\lambda^{-1}\partial_{y_k}H$,
$\dot y_k=-\lambda^{-1}\partial_{x_k}H$, as in \texttt{note.tex}. The function $f(d)=\ln\tanh(d/2)$ satisfies
$f''+\coth d\,f'=0$ for $d>0$ (since $f'=1/\sinh d$). So $G(d)=-\frac1{2\pi}\ln\tanh(d/2)$ is the Green's function of
$\Delta_{\mathbb H^2}$ that vanishes as $d\to\infty$.

\paragraph{Attribution (secondary only).} Montaldi--Tokieda~\cite[\S4.2]{MT} state that the image Green's function
$\log|z-w|^2/|1+\lambda z\bar w|^2$ ``on the hyperbolic plane $\lambda<0$ \dots\ reduces to the Hamiltonian adopted by
Kimura''. Montaldi--Nava-Gaxiola~\cite[eq.~(12)]{MNG} write Kimura's Hamiltonian as
$-\frac1{4\pi}\sum\Gamma_i\Gamma_j\ln\frac{\langle X_i,X_j\rangle+1}{\langle X_i,X_j\rangle-1}$ with $\langle X,Y\rangle=-\cosh d$,
which equals $-\frac1{4\pi}\sum\ln\tanh^2(d/2)$. Boatto~\cite{Boatto} uses the same Green's function on the hyperbolic
surface. We have read only the abstract of Kimura~\cite{Kimura}. Hence (\ref{H}) is ``the model attributed to Kimura'',
and we quote nothing from Kimura directly.

\paragraph{No Robin term (intrinsic remark).} $\mathbb H^2$ is homogeneous, so the regular part of $G$ at the diagonal,
measured in the intrinsic metric, is the constant $\lim_{d\to0}(G(d)+\frac1{2\pi}\ln d)=\frac1{2\pi}\ln2$. The
Robin (self-interaction) function is therefore constant, and (\ref{H}) has no one-body self-term. This contrasts with the flat
disk with a hard wall (\S\ref{sec:compare}), where the Robin function $\frac1{2\pi}\ln(1-|z|^2)$ is not constant and,
combined with the Euclidean area form, changes the dynamics. In conformal coordinates, a one-body term
$-\frac12\sum\ln(1-|z_k|^2)$ does appear in (\ref{eq:decomp}) below. That term comes from the $j\neq k$ image sums, not
from a self-interaction.

\paragraph{Relation to the sinh model.} Since $\sinh(d/2)=\tanh(d/2)\cosh(d/2)$,
$H_{\sinh}=-\sum\ln\sinh(d_{jk}/2)=H-\sum_{j<k}\ln\cosh(d_{jk}/2)$. A direct computation gives
$\Delta_{\mathbb H^2}\ln\cosh(d/2)=\frac12$. So the sinh model is the tanh model plus a uniform compensating background
vorticity attached to each vortex. This is the continuation to $\mathbb H^2$ of the sphere model, where Gauss's law forces such a
term. The sinh model is the $\lambda<0$ member of Montaldi--Tokieda's family~\cite[(1.12)]{MT}.

\subsection{Power-sum decomposition}
Let $P_p=\sum_j z_j^p$. Using $\ln|1-u|=-\operatorname{Re}\sum_{p\ge1}u^p/p$ and
$\sum_{j\ne k}(z_j\bar z_k)^p=|P_p|^2-\sum_j|z_j|^{2p}$, we get
\begin{equation}\label{eq:decomp}
H=-\sum_{j<k}\ln|z_j-z_k|\;-\;\frac12\sum_j\ln(1-|z_j|^2)\;-\;\frac12\sum_{p\ge1}\frac{|P_p|^2}{p}.
\end{equation}
The series converges absolutely on $\D^N$. The last term $\Phi:=-\frac12\sum_p|P_p|^2/p$ is the source of the
apparent intractability. On a ring it is diagonal in the Fourier modes.

\section{Fourier blocks}
\subsection{Coordinates and the relative equilibrium}
Let $w=e^{2\pi i/N}$ and write $z_j=\sqrt\xi\,w^j e^{s_j+i\theta_j}$, so the ring of Euclidean radius $\sqrt\xi$ is
$s=\theta=0$. Then $\omega=\sum_j\kappa(s_j)\,ds_j\wedge d\theta_j$ with $\kappa(s)=4\xi e^{2s}/(1-\xi e^{2s})^2$, and
$\kappa_0:=\kappa(0)=4\xi/(1-\xi)^2$. Set
\[
c:=\cosh r=\frac{1+\xi}{1-\xi},\qquad X:=\xi^N,\qquad
W_k:=\sum_{\substack{p\ge1\\ p\equiv k\ (N)}}p\,\xi^p=\frac{k\xi^k}{1-X}+\frac{N\xi^{k+N}}{(1-X)^2}\quad(1\le k\le N).
\]
In particular $W_N=NX/(1-X)^2$. Let $F=H-\Omega J$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:re}
The ring is a relative equilibrium for every $\xi\in(0,1)$, with
$\displaystyle \Omega\kappa_0=-\frac{N-1}2+\frac{\xi}{1-\xi}-\frac{NX}{1-X}$.
At the ring, $dF=0$ in all $2N$ variables.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
All $\theta$-derivatives vanish by the dihedral symmetry. For $s_j$: the pair term contributes
$-\frac{N-1}2$ (its linear part is $-\frac12\sum_{j<k}(s_j+s_k)$; see \texttt{note.tex}, \S2). The one-body part is
$V(s)=-\frac12\ln(1-u)-\Omega\frac{1+u}{1-u}$ with $u=\xi e^{2s}$, and $V'(0)=\frac{\xi}{1-\xi}-\Omega\kappa_0$. For $\Phi$, write
$|P_p|^2=\xi^p\sum_{j,k}w^{(j-k)p}e^{p\epsilon_j+p\bar\epsilon_k}$ with $\epsilon_j=s_j+i\theta_j$. Then
$\partial_{s_j}|P_p|^2\big|_0=2p\,\xi^p\operatorname{Re}\sum_kw^{(j-k)p}=2pN\xi^p[N\mid p]$, so
$\partial_{s_j}\Phi|_0=-N\sum_{q\ge1}X^q=-NX/(1-X)$. Setting the sum to zero gives $\Omega$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The block theorem}
\begin{theorem}[Fourier blocks; PROVED]\label{thm:blocks}
In the coordinates $(s,\theta)$, the Hessian of $F$ at the ring is block-circulant. On the Fourier mode
$(s_j,\theta_j)=(S,T)\,w^{mj}$, $1\le m\le N-1$, it acts as the Hermitian matrix
\[
M_m=\begin{pmatrix}a_m& i g_m\\ -ig_m& b_m\end{pmatrix},
\]
where
\begin{align*}
a_m&=(N-1)c-\tfrac12(c-1)^2-\tfrac{m(N-m)}2+\frac{2NcX}{1-X}-\frac{N^2X}{(1-X)^2}\\
&\quad-\frac N2\,\frac{m\xi^m+(N-m)\xi^{N-m}+(N-m)\xi^{N+m}+m\xi^{2N-m}}{(1-X)^2},\\
b_m&=\tfrac{m(N-m)}2+NW_N-\tfrac N2\,(W_m+W_{N-m}),\qquad
g_m=\tfrac N2\,(W_m-W_{N-m}).
\end{align*}
On mode $0$, $b_0=g_0=0$. All entries are rational functions of $\xi$ with rational coefficients. Moreover
$a_{N-m}=a_m$, $b_{N-m}=b_m$, $g_{N-m}=-g_m$, and $g_{N/2}=0$ for even $N$.
\end{theorem}
For comparison, the sinh model has $a_m=(N-1)(1+c^2)/2-m(N-m)/2$, $b_m=m(N-m)/2$ and $g_m=0$
(\texttt{note.tex}, Prop.~2).

\begin{proof}
Since $dF=0$ at the ring (Lemma~\ref{lem:re}), the Hessian is well defined. We add the three contributions in
(\ref{eq:decomp}) and the term $-\Omega J$.

\emph{Pair term.} As in \texttt{note.tex} (Lemma~1 and the expansion before it), $-\sum_{j<k}\ln|z_j-z_k|$ has
Hessian $-\frac{m(N-m)}2$ in $ss$ and $+\frac{m(N-m)}2$ in $\theta\theta$ on mode $m$, with no $s\theta$ coupling.

\emph{One-body terms.} $V(s)$ from the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:re} does not depend on $\theta$. Using
$\frac{d}{ds}=2u\frac{d}{du}$,
\[
V''(0)=\frac{2\xi}{(1-\xi)^2}-\frac{8\Omega\xi(1+\xi)}{(1-\xi)^3}
=\frac{2\xi}{(1-\xi)^2}-2c\,\Omega\kappa_0 .
\]
Substituting $\Omega\kappa_0$ and using $c-1=2\xi/(1-\xi)$, so that $\frac{2\xi}{(1-\xi)^2}-\frac{2c\xi}{1-\xi}=-\frac12(c-1)^2$,
gives $V''(0)=(N-1)c-\frac12(c-1)^2+\frac{2NcX}{1-X}$. This term is the same on every mode and lies in the $ss$ entry.

\emph{Power sums.} The second-order part of $e^{p\epsilon_j+p\bar\epsilon_k}$ is
$\frac{p^2}2(\epsilon_j^2+2\epsilon_j\bar\epsilon_k+\bar\epsilon_k^2)$. Summing with the weight $w^{(j-k)p}$:
(i) the squares give $p^2N[N\mid p]\sum_j\operatorname{Re}\epsilon_j^2=p^2N[N\mid p]\sum_j(s_j^2-\theta_j^2)$;
(ii) the cross term gives $p^2\big|\sum_jw^{jp}\epsilon_j\big|^2$. Hence the quadratic part of $\Phi$ is
\[
\Phi_2=-\frac N2W_N\sum_j(s_j^2-\theta_j^2)\;-\;\frac12\sum_{p\ge1}p\,\xi^p\Big|\sum_jw^{jp}(s_j+i\theta_j)\Big|^2 .
\]
The first piece contributes $-NW_N$ to $ss$ and $+NW_N$ to $\theta\theta$. For the second, expand
$|\sum_jw^{jp}\epsilon_j|^2$ as a real quadratic form. It equals $\sum_{j,k}\cos\phi_{jk}(s_js_k+\theta_j\theta_k)+2\sum_{j,k}\sin\phi_{jk}\,s_j\theta_k$
with $\phi_{jk}=2\pi(j-k)p/N$. Its Hessian has circulant blocks $2\cos\phi_{jk}$ ($ss$, $\theta\theta$) and $2\sin\phi_{jk}$ ($s\theta$).
On the vector $w^{mk}$ their symbols are $N([p\equiv m]+[p\equiv-m])$ and $-iN([p\equiv m]-[p\equiv-m])$, using
$\sum_kw^{(j-k)p}w^{mk}=Nw^{mj}[p\equiv m]$. Multiplying by $-\frac12p\xi^p$ and summing over $p$ gives
$-\frac N2(W_m+W_{N-m})$ on both diagonal entries and $+\frac{iN}2(W_m-W_{N-m})=ig_m$ in the $s\theta$ entry.

Adding the three pieces gives $a_m$, $b_m$ and $g_m$. In particular,
$a_m=V''(0)-\frac{m(N-m)}2-NW_N-\frac N2(W_m+W_{N-m})$, and expanding $W_m+W_{N-m}$ over the common denominator
$(1-X)^2$ gives the bracket in the statement. For $m=0$, $W_0$ should be read as $W_N$, which gives $b_0=NW_N-NW_N=0$.
The symmetries follow from $m\leftrightarrow N-m$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Checks.} The blocks equal the Fourier symbol of the exact Cartesian Hessian of $F$ (sympy second
derivatives, with $\Omega$ solved numerically) to $\le2\cdot10^{-48}$. The grid was $N\in\{3,5,7,8,9,10,12,13,16\}$ and
$\xi\in\{0.02,0.09,0.3,0.75\}$ (\texttt{check\_jacobian.out}). An independent second reader built a Cartesian Hessian at

form of $a_m$ agrees exactly with the block formula for $N=3..16$ and all $m$ (\texttt{identities.out}).

\section{Linearisation and stability criteria}
\begin{proposition}[PROVED]\label{prop:lin}
On mode $m$ the linearised flow is $\kappa_0(\dot S,\dot T)^{\!\top}=\begin{psmallmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{psmallmatrix}M_m(S,T)^{\!\top}$.
Its eigenvalues are
\[
\kappa_0\sigma=-ig_m\pm\sqrt{-a_mb_m}.
\]
Hence the ring is spectrally stable iff $a_mb_m\ge0$ for all $1\le m\le N-1$. The Hessian of $F$ is positive definite on
the non-rotational modes $1\le m\le N-1$ iff $a_m>0$ and $a_mb_m-g_m^2>0$ for all such $m$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\det\big(\begin{psmallmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{psmallmatrix}M_m-\lambda\big)=(\lambda+ig_m)^2+a_mb_m$. A $2\times2$ Hermitian
matrix is positive definite iff its $(1,1)$ entry and its determinant $a_mb_m-g_m^2$ are positive. Mode $0$ (rotation, and
change of $\xi$ along the family) carries $b_0=0$ and is excluded.
\end{proof}
So the coupling $g_m$ only shifts the frequencies; it does not affect spectral stability. It does affect
definiteness, and this is the origin of the odd-$N$ window in \S\ref{sec:krein}.

\begin{remark}[energy--momentum, ``modulo $SO(2,1)$'']\label{rem:em}
The variation $dJ$ annihilates every mode $m\ne0$. Hence $\ker dJ\subset(\text{modes }1..N-1)\oplus\mathbb R\,\partial_\theta^{(0)}$,
and positive definiteness on modes $1..N-1$ implies definiteness of $d^2F$ on $\ker dJ$ modulo the rotation orbit.
The full symmetry group of (\ref{H}) is the isometry group $PSL(2,\mathbb R)\cong SO(2,1)_0$. Its momentum map has
three components, and the modes $m=\pm1$ are tangent to the non-compact (boost) directions. We therefore state any
Lyapunov conclusion only \emph{modulo $SO(2,1)$}: up to motion along the group orbit. We do not claim stability modulo
$SO(2)$ alone. We did not verify the hypotheses of a specific energy--momentum theorem (for example Montaldi--Tokieda~\cite[Thm.~2]{MT},
which is stated for the sinh model) for the tanh model.
\end{remark}

\section{Reciprocity and the threshold equation}\label{sec:recip}
\begin{proposition}[reciprocity; PROVED for all $N,m$]\label{prop:recip}
As rational functions of $\xi$, under the map $\xi\mapsto1/\xi$,
\[
a_m(1/\xi)=a_m(\xi),\qquad b_m(1/\xi)=b_m(\xi),\qquad g_m(1/\xi)=-g_m(\xi).
\]
Hence $M_m(1/\xi)=\overline{M_m(\xi)}$, and $a_mb_m$ and $a_mb_m-g_m^2$ are invariant.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
For $1\le k\le N-1$ the closed form of $W_k$ gives
$W_k(1/\xi)=\xi^{N-k}\frac{(N-k)+kX}{(1-X)^2}=W_{N-k}(\xi)$, and $W_N(1/\xi)=W_N(\xi)$ since $X/(1-X)^2$ is invariant under
$X\mapsto1/X$. So $W_m+W_{N-m}$ is invariant and $W_m-W_{N-m}$ changes sign, which gives the claims for $b_m$ and $g_m$.
For $a_m$: under $\xi\mapsto1/\xi$ we have $c\mapsto-c$ and $X\mapsto1/X$. The one-body part changes as follows:
$(N-1)c\mapsto-(N-1)c$; $-\frac12(c-1)^2\mapsto-\frac12(c+1)^2$, a change of $-2c$; and
$\frac{2NcX}{1-X}\mapsto\frac{-2Nc/X}{1-1/X}=\frac{2Nc}{1-X}$, a change of $+2Nc$. The total change is
$-2(N-1)c-2c+2Nc=0$. The term $N^2X/(1-X)^2$ is invariant. In the last fraction, the numerator is multiplied by
$\xi^{-2N}$ (its exponents $m,N-m,N+m,2N-m$ are permuted by $e\mapsto2N-e$) and so is $(1-X)^2$. The term
$m(N-m)/2$ is constant.
\end{proof}
A symbolic check treating $\xi$, $X=\xi^N$ and $t=\xi^m$ as independent symbols confirms the identity for $a_m$
(\texttt{review/palin\_symbolic.py}); Lean checks it too (\texttt{aBlock\_recip}).

\begin{remark}[geometric meaning]
The disk model and its exterior $\{|z|>1\}\cup\{\infty\}$, each with the metric $4|dz|^2/(1-|z|^2)^2$, are two copies of
$\mathbb H^2$, exchanged by the inversion $\iota(z)=1/\bar z$ in the boundary circle; $\iota$ is an isometry between them. It
preserves the pair term, since $\big|\frac{1/\bar z-1/\bar w}{1-1/(\bar z w)}\big|=\frac{|z-w|}{|1-z\bar w|}$, and it maps the
ring of Euclidean radius $\sqrt\xi$ to the ring of radius $1/\sqrt\xi$. So the continuation of the blocks to $\xi>1$
describes the same hyperbolic ring in the exterior chart, and $1/\xi_*$ is the threshold seen from that chart, not a
second physical threshold. (\texttt{derivation.txt} gives only the algebraic proof above. We record this interpretation
as a remark and do not use it in any proof. The sign change of $g_m$ reflects the fact that $\iota$ reverses
orientation.)
\end{remark}

Clearing denominators, $A_m:=2(1-\xi)^2(1-X)^2a_m\in\mathbb Z[\xi]$ satisfies
$A_m(1/\xi)=\xi^{-2N-2}A_m(\xi)$, so $A_m$ is self-reciprocal. We write
$B_m:=2(1-X)^2b_m$, $G_m:=2(1-X)^2g_m$ and $D_m:=A_mB_m-(1-\xi)^2G_m^2=4(1-\xi)^2(1-X)^4(a_mb_m-g_m^2)$.
On $(0,1)$ the signs of $a_m$, $b_m$ and $a_mb_m-g_m^2$ are those of $A_m$, $B_m$ and $D_m$.

\paragraph{Threshold equation.} If $b_m>0$ on $(0,1)$ for all $m$ (certified for $N\le16$, \S\ref{sec:cert}), instability
occurs only through the radial entries. The spectral threshold $\xi_*(N)$ is then the largest root in $(0,1)$ of
$\prod_m a_m$. For $N\le16$ this is the unique root of $a_{\lfloor N/2\rfloor}$ in $(0,1)$. For even $N$ and $m=N/2$, the bracket
and the $N^2X$ term combine. With
\[
y:=\xi^{N/2}=\tanh^N(r/2),
\]
the threshold equation $a_{N/2}=0$ reads
\begin{equation}\label{eq:even}
(N-1)c-\frac{(c-1)^2}2+\frac{2Ncy^2}{1-y^2}=\frac{N^2}8+\frac{N^2y}{2(1-y)^2},\qquad c=\frac{1+\xi}{1-\xi}.
\end{equation}
(Use $m\xi^m+(N-m)\xi^{N-m}=Ny$, $(N-m)\xi^{N+m}+m\xi^{2N-m}=Ny^3$, and $y(1+y^2)+2y^2=y(1+y)^2$.) For odd $N$ we use the
general $a_m$ with $m=(N-1)/2$. In the sinh model the right-hand side is the same Thomson term $N^2/8$ without the
image correction, and the left-hand side is $(N-1)(1+c^2)/2$.

\paragraph{Algebraicity is automatic; the structure is not.} Since the blocks are rational in $\xi$ with rational
coefficients, every threshold is a root of an integer polynomial, so ``the thresholds are algebraic'' carries no
information by itself. What is informative is the structure: the self-reciprocal numerators $A_m$, the irreducible
palindromic factor that carries $\xi_*$, its degree, and its leading and constant coefficients (\S\ref{sec:cert}).

\paragraph{The natural invariant.} By reciprocity, if the minimal polynomial $f$ of $\xi_*$ is palindromic of degree $n$,
then $f(x)=x^{n/2}g(x+1/x)$, and $C:=\xi_*+\xi_*^{-1}$ has degree $n/2$ with minimal polynomial $g$. Since $\xi=\tanh^2(r/2)$,
\[
C=\xi_*+\xi_*^{-1}=2+\frac4{\sinh^2r_*}=\frac{2(\cosh^2r_*+1)}{\cosh^2r_*-1}.
\]
So $\cosh^2r_*$ generates the same field as $C$. In the sinh model, $C=2+4/(q-1)$ is rational (\texttt{note.tex}, \S4).

\section{Certified thresholds for $N\le16$}\label{sec:cert}
\paragraph{Method} (\texttt{certify\_v2.py} $\to$ \texttt{certify\_v2.out}). For each $N\le16$ and $1\le m\le\lfloor N/2\rfloor$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item Form the exact integer polynomials $A_m,B_m,D_m$ (sympy, from Thm.~\ref{thm:blocks}).
\item Isolate all real roots in $(0,1)$ of the square-free parts with exact rational real-root isolation, after removing
the factors $\xi$ and $\xi-1$. This shows: $B_m$ has no root and $B_m(\frac12)>0$; every $A_m$ with
$m\ne\lfloor N/2\rfloor$ has all its roots strictly below the isolating interval of $A_{\lfloor N/2\rfloor}$; and
$A_{\lfloor N/2\rfloor}$ has exactly one root in $(0,1)$.
\item Factor $A_{\rm crit}$ over $\mathbb Q$ (sympy) and select the unique irreducible factor $f$ with a root in $(0,1)$.
Its irreducibility is certified separately with PARI/GP (\S\ref{sec:irred}). Refine an exact rational isolating interval $[\alpha,\beta]$ for its root, of width $<10^{-45}$.
\item Assert exactly that $A_{\rm crit}(\alpha)<0<A_{\rm crit}(\beta)$. With step~2 this proves $a_{\rm crit}<0$ on
$(0,\xi_*)$ and $a_m>0$ for all $m$ on $(\xi_*,1)$.
\item Compute Arb balls for the roots of $f$ (python-flint 0.9, 400 bits). Select the real root by exact tests only:
the real roots returned by \texttt{arb\_fmpz\_poly\_complex\_roots} have imaginary part exactly $0$. Assert that the
whole ball $[\mathrm{mid}-\mathrm{rad},\mathrm{mid}+\mathrm{rad}]$, converted exactly to rationals, lies inside $[\alpha,\beta]$.
\item For the energy--momentum threshold, take the largest root of $\prod_mD_m$ in $(0,1)$. Isolate it exactly, assert that it
is separated from the roots of the other $D_m$, and compare it exactly with $[\alpha,\beta]$.
\end{enumerate}
No floating-point comparison enters any pass/fail decision. The first version (\texttt{certify.py}) selected the Arb root
by a float comparison of its midpoint. The second reader flagged this, and version~2 replaces it. All values and
polynomials in v1 and v2 agree.

\subsection{Irreducibility (CERTIFIED by PARI/GP)}\label{sec:irred}
Scripts and output are in \texttt{tanh/irreducibility/}. The polynomials $f_N$ ($8\le N\le16$) are read from
\texttt{lean/Havelock/TanhAlgebra.lean}; \texttt{extract.py} recomputes them from \texttt{exact.py} (factor
$A_{\lfloor N/2\rfloor}$ and take the factor with a root in $(0,1)$) and finds the two sources identical. With PARI/GP 2.15.4
(\texttt{irreducibility.gp}) we check, for every $N$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \texttt{polisirreducible}$(f_N)=1$, content $1$, $f_N$ palindromic, and degree and leading coefficient as in
Table~\ref{tab:cert} and (\ref{eq:deg}).
\item A second, independent witness. For the first six primes $p\nmid\operatorname{lead}(f_N)\operatorname{disc}(f_N)$
we record the degrees of the irreducible factors of $f_N$ mod $p$. A factorisation over $\mathbb Q$ into factors of
degrees $d$ and $n-d$ would give a sub-multiset of each pattern summing to $d$. For every $N$ no $0<d<n$ is attained by
all six patterns, so $f_N$ is irreducible. For example, for $N=8$ the patterns mod $3,5,7$ are $(5,5)$, $(2,8)$, $(10)$.
\item The trace polynomial $g_N$ with $f_N(x)=x^{n/2}g_N(x+1/x)$ is irreducible and agrees with \texttt{certify\_v2.out};
the discriminants agree too.
\end{enumerate}
Since $f_N$ is irreducible and primitive, it is (up to sign) the minimal polynomial of $\xi_*(N)$ over $\mathbb Z$. If
$\operatorname{lead}(f_N)\ne\pm1$, then by Gauss's lemma the monic minimal polynomial $f_N/\operatorname{lead}(f_N)$ is not
in $\mathbb Z[x]$, so $\xi_*(N)$ is not an algebraic integer.

\emph{Mod-$p$ certificates.} If $f_N$ is irreducible mod a prime $p\nmid\operatorname{lead}(f_N)$, then $f_N$ is irreducible
over $\mathbb Q$. Such a prime exists for every $N$ here. The smallest ones are
$p=7,13,107,17,11,101,3,29,163$ for $N=8,\dots,16$. A certificate of this kind could be checked in Lean; we have not
done so.

\subsection{$N=8$: an algebraic unit}
\begin{corollary}[CERTIFIED; irreducibility by PARI/GP]
$\xi_*(8)$ is an algebraic unit of degree $10$ with minimal polynomial
\[
x^{10}-12x^9-37x^8-72x^7-84x^6-88x^5-84x^4-72x^3-37x^2-12x+1 .
\]
$C=\xi_*+\xi_*^{-1}=2+4/\sinh^2r_*$ has minimal polynomial $C^5-12C^4-42C^3-24C^2+32C+32$.
For $9\le N\le16$ the leading coefficients of the (irreducible, primitive) $f_N$ are $4,7,10,7,18,23,28,17$, so
$\xi_*(N)$ is not an algebraic integer.
\end{corollary}
The polynomial is irreducible (\S\ref{sec:irred}), monic, with constant term $1$, so $\xi_*(8)$ is a unit. The second reader recovered the same polynomial
independently by PSLQ from a 110-digit value and confirmed its irreducibility (\texttt{review/minpoly8.py}). Numerically, its
only real roots are $\xi_*(8)$ and $1/\xi_*(8)\approx14.846$. Four conjugates lie on $|x|=1$ and four do not, so $\xi_*(8)$ is
neither Pisot nor Salem. In the sinh model $\xi_*(8)=8-3\sqrt7$ is also a unit, of degree $2$. We do not know whether the
coincidence has a reason.

\begin{conjecture}\label{conj:deg}
For every $N\ge8$: the factor of $A_{\lfloor N/2\rfloor}$
carrying $\xi_*$ is irreducible and palindromic, so that $1/\xi_*$ is a Galois conjugate of $\xi_*$; and its degree is given by
(\ref{eq:deg}).
\end{conjecture}
Reciprocity alone shows that $1/\xi_*$ is a root of $A_{\lfloor N/2\rfloor}$. It does not show that $1/\xi_*$ is a conjugate
of $\xi_*$. That needs irreducibility of the factor, which is why palindromy of the minimal polynomial is certified
only for $N\le16$. An earlier version of this conjecture also asserted $b_m>0$ and critical mode $\lfloor N/2\rfloor$
for every $N$; these are now proved (Lemmas~\ref{lem:bpos} and~\ref{lem:crit}).

\section{Odd $N$: the Krein window}\label{sec:krein}
For even $N$, $g_{N/2}=0$, so $D_{N/2}=A_{N/2}B_{N/2}$ and the two thresholds coincide. This is certified: the
same irreducible factor carries both. For odd $N$, $g_{\rm crit}\ne0$. At $\xi_*$ we have $a_{\rm crit}b_{\rm crit}-g_{\rm crit}^2=-g_{\rm crit}^2<0$, and
the Hessian becomes definite only at a larger value $\xi_{\rm EM}$.

\begin{proposition}[CERTIFIED, odd $9\le N\le15$]\label{prop:kreincert}
$\xi_{\rm EM}(N)>\xi_*(N)$ strictly (disjoint exact rational intervals). The gaps are
$3.220\cdot10^{-8}$ $(N=9)$, $1.244\cdot10^{-7}$ $(11)$, $1.777\cdot10^{-7}$ $(13)$ and $1.886\cdot10^{-7}$ $(15)$.
On $(\xi_*,\xi_{\rm EM})$ the ring is linearly (spectrally) stable, with distinct purely imaginary eigenvalues, but the
critical block $M_{\rm crit}$ has one positive and one negative eigenvalue. Its nonlinear stability there is
undecided.
\end{proposition}
In the window the two frequencies of the critical block carry opposite Krein signatures. The second reader confirmed this
on the coordinate-free Cartesian Hessian (count of negative eigenvalues, Krein signs of the eigenvectors;
\texttt{review/krein\_em.py}), so it is not a coordinate artefact. The energy--momentum method gives no conclusion there:
the ring is linearly (spectrally) stable, and nonlinear stability is \textbf{undecided}. In the sinh model $g_m\equiv0$, and no such window exists.

\section{Large $N$: the continuum limit and a critical spacing}\label{sec:asym}
This section proves that $\cosh r_*/N$ converges, identifies the limit, and interprets it. The key is an exact identity:
in a suitable radial variable the blocks of Thm.~\ref{thm:blocks} are, up to a term that does not depend on $m$, a function
of $m/N$ alone. Throughout, put
\[
\tau:=-\tfrac N2\ln\xi=N\operatorname{artanh}\tfrac1c\in(0,\infty),\qquad x:=1-\tfrac{2m}N\in(-1,1),\qquad
\theta:=\tfrac mN=\tfrac{1-x}2,
\]
so that $\xi^N=e^{-2\tau}$, $c=\cosh r=\coth(\tau/N)$, and $\tau\to0$ as $r\to\infty$. Let
$Q(x,\tau):=\cosh\tau\cosh(x\tau)-x\sinh\tau\sinh(x\tau)$, and let $\mathcal L(\eta):=\coth\eta-1/\eta$ be the Langevin
function.

\begin{theorem}[exact symbol; PROVED]\label{thm:symbol}
For every $N\ge2$, $1\le m\le N-1$ and $\xi\in(0,1)$, with $k:=c/N=\coth(\tau/N)/N$,
\begin{align*}
\frac{b_m}{N^2}&=\beta(x,\tau):=\frac{1-x^2}8+\frac{1-Q(x,\tau)}{4\sinh^2\tau},\\
\frac{a_m}{N^2}&=E_N(\tau)-\frac1{4\sinh^2\tau}-\psi(x,\tau),\qquad
\psi(x,\tau):=\frac{1-x^2}8+\frac{Q(x,\tau)}{4\sinh^2\tau},\qquad E_N(\tau):=k\coth\tau-\frac{k^2}2-\frac1{2N^2},\\
\frac{g_m}{N^2}&=\gamma(x,\tau):=\frac12\Big[\frac{\theta e^{x\tau}-(1-\theta)e^{-x\tau}}{2\sinh\tau}
+\frac{e^{-\tau}\sinh(x\tau)}{2\sinh^2\tau}\Big].
\end{align*}
The dependence on $m$ enters only through $x$, and in $a_m$ only through $\psi$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Substitute $\xi^j=e^{-2j\tau/N}$, so $\xi^m=e^{-(1-x)\tau}$, $\xi^{N-m}=e^{-(1+x)\tau}$, $1-X=2e^{-\tau}\sinh\tau$. From the
closed form of $W_k$, $W_m/N=\theta e^{x\tau}/(2\sinh\tau)+e^{-\tau}e^{x\tau}/(4\sinh^2\tau)$ and
$W_{N-m}/N=(1-\theta)e^{-x\tau}/(2\sinh\tau)+e^{-\tau}e^{-x\tau}/(4\sinh^2\tau)$, and $NW_N=N^2/(4\sinh^2\tau)$. Since
$\theta e^{x\tau}+(1-\theta)e^{-x\tau}=\cosh(x\tau)-x\sinh(x\tau)$ and $\sinh\tau+e^{-\tau}=\cosh\tau$,
\[
\frac{W_m+W_{N-m}}N=\frac{\sinh\tau\,(\cosh x\tau-x\sinh x\tau)+e^{-\tau}\cosh x\tau}{2\sinh^2\tau}=\frac{Q(x,\tau)}{2\sinh^2\tau}.
\]
With $m(N-m)=N^2(1-x^2)/4$ this gives $b_m$ and $g_m$. For $a_m$ use the form
$a_m=V''(0)-\frac{m(N-m)}2-NW_N-\frac N2(W_m+W_{N-m})$ from the proof of Thm.~\ref{thm:blocks}, where
$V''(0)=(N-1)c-\frac12(c-1)^2+\frac{2NcX}{1-X}=Nc\,\frac{1+X}{1-X}-\frac{c^2}2-\frac12$ and $\frac{1+X}{1-X}=\coth\tau$.
\end{proof}
The identity holds exactly; \texttt{large\_n.py} confirms it against \texttt{blocks.py} at 400 random $(N,m,\xi)$, $N\le300$, to
$8\cdot10^{-59}$.

\begin{lemma}[tangential stiffness; PROVED]\label{lem:bpos}
Put $a=(1+x)\tau$, $b=(1-x)\tau$ and $f(t)=\sinh^2(t/2)/t$. Then
\[
\beta(x,\tau)=\frac{ab}{4\tau\sinh^2\tau}\big[f(a+b)-f(a)-f(b)\big]>0\qquad(|x|<1,\ \tau>0).
\]
Hence $b_m>0$ for every $N\ge2$, every $1\le m\le N-1$ and every radius.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$1-x=b/\tau$, $1+x=a/\tau$ and $a+b=2\tau$ give $2Q=(b\cosh a+a\cosh b)/\tau=2+2\big(b\sinh^2\frac a2+a\sinh^2\frac b2\big)/\tau$ and
$(1-x^2)\sinh^2\tau=ab\sinh^2\tau/\tau^2=2ab\,f(a+b)/\tau$. Substituting into $8\sinh^2\tau\,\beta=(1-x^2)\sinh^2\tau+2-2Q$ gives the
formula. The function $f(t)/t=(\sinh(t/2)/t)^2$ is strictly increasing, so
$f(a+b)=a\frac{f(a+b)}{a+b}+b\frac{f(a+b)}{a+b}>a\frac{f(a)}a+b\frac{f(b)}b=f(a)+f(b)$ for $a,b>0$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[critical mode; PROVED]\label{lem:crit}
For every $\tau>0$, $\psi(\cdot,\tau)$ is even and strictly decreasing on $[0,1]$. Hence, for every $N\ge2$ and every radius,
$a_m\ge a_{\lfloor N/2\rfloor}$, with equality only for $m\in\{\lfloor N/2\rfloor,N-\lfloor N/2\rfloor\}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Evenness is clear. Write $h=x\tau\in(0,\tau]$, $T=\tau\coth\tau$ and $S(t)=\sinh t/t$. A direct computation gives
\[
4\sinh^2\tau\;\partial_x\psi=\sinh\tau\;v(h),\qquad v(h):=(T-1)\sinh h-h\cosh h-h\,S(\tau),
\]
and $v(h)/\sinh h=(T-1)-h\coth h-S(\tau)/S(h)$. So we must show $T-1-h\coth h<S(\tau)/S(h)$ for $0<h\le\tau$. Let
$\lambda(t)=\ln S(t)$, so $\lambda'=\coth t-1/t$, $\lambda''=t^{-2}-\sinh^{-2}t\ge0$, and $(t\coth t)'=\lambda'+t\lambda''$.
With $D:=\lambda(\tau)-\lambda(h)\ge0$ and $I:=\int_h^\tau t\lambda''(t)\,dt\ge0$ we have $T-h\coth h=D+I$, and the claim
becomes
\[
I<1+e^D-D .
\]
The right side is increasing in $D\ge0$ and exceeds $2$ when $h<\tau$ (then $D>0$); at $h=\tau$, $I=0$. We use
$\lambda''(t)\le\min(\frac13,t^{-2})$. (The bound $\frac13$ is $3(\sinh^2t-t^2)\le t^2\sinh^2t$; compare the Taylor
coefficients of $t^{2j}$, which are $3\cdot2^{2j-1}/(2j)!$ and $2^{2j-3}/(2j-2)!$, with ratio $(2j)(2j-1)/12\ge1$ for $j\ge2$.)
Hence $I\le\int_0^\tau t\min(\frac13,t^{-2})\,dt$, which is $\tau^2/6$ for $\tau\le\sqrt3$ and $\frac12+\ln(\tau/\sqrt3)$
otherwise; both are $\le2$ when $\tau\le\sqrt3\,e^{3/2}\approx7.76$. For $\tau>\sqrt3\,e^{3/2}$: if $h\ge\tau/2$ then
$I\le\int_{\tau/2}^\tau dt/t=\ln2<2$; if $h<\tau/2$ then $e^D\ge S(\tau)/S(\tau/2)=\cosh(\tau/2)$, so the right side is at least
$1+\frac12e^{\tau/2}-\frac\tau2$, which exceeds $\frac12+\ln(\tau/\sqrt3)\ge I$ for $\tau\ge7.76$. So $\partial_x\psi<0$ on $(0,1]$.
The statement about $a_m$ follows from Thm.~\ref{thm:symbol}, since $|x|=|1-2m/N|$ is smallest at $m=\lfloor N/2\rfloor$ and
$N-\lfloor N/2\rfloor$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[PROVED, all $N$]\label{cor:alln}
For every $N\ge2$ and every radius, the ring is spectrally stable if and only if $a_{\lfloor N/2\rfloor}\ge0$; when
$a_{\lfloor N/2\rfloor}<0$ the modes $m=\lfloor N/2\rfloor$ and $N-\lfloor N/2\rfloor$ grow exponentially. This removes the
restriction $N\le16$ from the statements ``$b_m>0$'' and ``the critical mode is $\lfloor N/2\rfloor$'' of
Thm.~\ref{thm:cert}(b); an earlier version of Conjecture~\ref{conj:deg} conjectured them for all $N$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
Prop.~\ref{prop:lin} with Lemmas~\ref{lem:bpos} and~\ref{lem:crit}.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[even $N$: one threshold; PROVED]\label{prop:evenN}
Let $N$ be even, $\eta=\tau/2$ and $\mu=\frac2N\coth\frac{2\eta}N$. Then
\[
\frac{8a_{N/2}}{N^2}=2\mu\tanh\eta-(\coth\eta-\mu)^2-\frac4{N^2},
\]
which for $N\ge4$ is strictly decreasing in $\eta$, from $2-4/N^2$ ($r\to\infty$) to $-(N^2-8N+8)/N^2$ ($r\to0$). Hence
every even $N\le6$ is stable at every radius, and every even $N\ge8$ has exactly one spectral threshold, at a simple
sign change of $a_{N/2}$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
At $x=0$, $\psi+\frac1{4\sinh^2\tau}=\frac18+\frac1{8\sinh^2\eta}$; with $\coth\tau=\frac12(\coth\eta+\tanh\eta)$, $k=\mu/2$ and
$\sinh^{-2}\eta=\coth^2\eta-1$ the formula follows from Thm.~\ref{thm:symbol}. For $N=2$, $\mu=\coth\eta$ and the
expression is identically $1$, so the ring is stable at every radius. For $N\ge4$, with $v=2\eta/N$ and $q=N/2>1$,
$\mu\tanh\eta=\frac2N\tanh(qv)/\tanh v$, and $\frac d{dv}\ln\frac{\tanh(qv)}{\tanh v}=\frac{2q}{\sinh2qv}-\frac2{\sinh2v}<0$,
because $t/\sinh t$ is strictly decreasing. Since $t\coth t$ is increasing, $\mu\le\coth\eta$; and
$\frac d{d\eta}(\coth\eta-\mu)=\frac{(2/N)^2}{\sinh^2(2\eta/N)}-\frac1{\sinh^2\eta}\ge0$, again because $t/\sinh t$ decreases. So
$(\coth\eta-\mu)^2$ is non-decreasing and the whole expression is strictly decreasing. The limits are direct.
\end{proof}
At $r\to0$ the limit $-(N^2-8N+8)/N^2$ is the planar (Thomson--Havelock) value, negative exactly for $N\ge7$. For odd
$N$ the same computation gives $8a_{(N-1)/2}/N^2=P_{12}+P_3-3/N^2$, where $P_{12}=2\mu\tanh\eta-(\coth\eta-\mu)^2$ and
$P_3=\big[\frac2N\sinh v\sinh Nv-2\cosh Nv(\cosh v-1)\big]/\sinh^2Nv$, $v=\tau/N$; its planar limit is $-(N-1)(N-7)/N^2$. The
term $P_3$ is not monotone, but where it increases its slope is below $0.61\%$ of $|P_{12}'|$ for the sampled odd
$N\in\{9,11,15,21,51,101,1001\}$, and below $37.5\%$ for $N=3$ (\texttt{large\_n.out}, NUMERICAL). So for odd $N$ the threshold is unique numerically; we have not proved it.

\paragraph{The limit.} Let $E_\infty(\tau)=\coth\tau/\tau-1/(2\tau^2)$ and
$\Phi(\tau):=E_\infty(\tau)-\frac1{4\sinh^2\tau}-\psi(0,\tau)$, the $N\to\infty$ limit of $a_{\lfloor N/2\rfloor}/N^2$. With $\eta=\tau/2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:phi}
8\,\Phi=\frac{2\tanh\eta}\eta-\mathcal L(\eta)^2 .
\end{equation}
Since $(\tanh\eta/\eta)'=(\eta-\sinh\eta\cosh\eta)/(\eta^2\cosh^2\eta)<0$ and $(\mathcal L^2)'=2\mathcal L\mathcal L'>0$
($\mathcal L>0$, $\mathcal L'=\eta^{-2}-\sinh^{-2}\eta>0$), we have $\Phi'<0$ everywhere: $\Phi$ decreases strictly
from $\frac14$ at $\tau=0$ to $-\frac18$ at $\tau=\infty$. Let $\eta_\infty$ be its unique zero:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:kappa}
\Big(\coth\eta_\infty-\frac1{\eta_\infty}\Big)^2=\frac{2\tanh\eta_\infty}{\eta_\infty},\qquad
\eta_\infty=3.7226696224745093436\ldots,\qquad \kappa_\infty:=\frac1{2\eta_\infty}=0.1343122142726281390560\ldots
\end{equation}
This is the constant of the earlier draft: its defining equation, in $\kappa=1/\tau$, is $\Phi(1/\kappa)=0$ rewritten with
$u=e^{-1/\kappa}$. Dropping the exponentially small terms ($\tanh\eta,\coth\eta\to1$) turns (\ref{eq:kappa}) into
$\eta^2-4\eta+1=0$, so $\eta_\infty\approx2+\sqrt3$ (off by $9.4\cdot10^{-3}$) and $\kappa_\infty\approx1-\sqrt3/2$ (off by
$3.4\cdot10^{-4}$).

\begin{theorem}[large-$N$ limit; PROVED]\label{thm:largeN}
Let $r_*(N)$ be the spectral threshold radius, i.e.\ $\xi_*(N)=\inf\{\xi_0:\text{the ring is spectrally stable for all }
\xi\in(\xi_0,1)\}$. Then, for both parities,
\[
\cosh r_*(N)=\kappa_\infty N+O(1/N),\qquad
\ell_N:=\frac{2\pi\sinh r_*(N)}N=\ell_*+O(N^{-2}),\qquad \ell_*:=2\pi\kappa_\infty=\frac\pi{\eta_\infty}=0.8439085\ldots
\]
In words: at threshold, the arc length along the ring between neighbouring vortices tends to $\ell_*\approx0.844$
curvature radii. (The geodesic distance between neighbours tends to $2\operatorname{arsinh}(\ell_*/2)\approx0.821$.)
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Let $\tau_*(N)=-\frac N2\ln\xi_*(N)$. By Thm.~\ref{thm:symbol},
$a_{\lfloor N/2\rfloor}/N^2=\Phi(\tau)+\Delta_N(\tau)+\varepsilon_N(\tau)$ with $\Delta_N=E_N-E_\infty$ and
$\varepsilon_N=\psi(0,\tau)-\psi(x_N,\tau)$, where $x_N=0$ ($N$ even) or $1/N$ ($N$ odd). By Lemma~\ref{lem:crit},
$\varepsilon_N\ge0$; by Taylor's theorem $\varepsilon_N(\tau)\le\frac1{2N^2}\max_{|x|\le1/N}|\psi_{xx}(x,\tau)|$, bounded by
$C/N^2$ for $\tau$ in a compact set. For $\Delta_N$, put $\delta=k-1/\tau=\tau^{-1}(u\coth u-1)$, $u=\tau/N$; then
$0<\delta<\tau/(3N^2)$ and $\Delta_N=\delta(\coth\tau-\frac1\tau-\frac\delta2)-\frac1{2N^2}$. Since $0<\coth\tau-1/\tau<1$,
$-\frac1{2N^2}-\frac{\delta^2}2\le\Delta_N\le\frac\tau{3N^2}$, so $|\Delta_N|\le C_T/N^2$ on $(0,T]$, uniformly down to $\tau\to0$.
Fix $T>\tau_\infty=2\eta_\infty$ and let $c_0=\frac12|\Phi'(\tau_\infty)|>0$. For $0<d\le d_0$ we have $\Phi(\tau_\infty-d)\ge c_0d$
and $\Phi(\tau_\infty+d)\le-c_0d$. Take $d=K/N^2$ with $K$ large. On $(0,\tau_\infty-d]$,
$a_{\lfloor N/2\rfloor}/N^2\ge c_0d-C_T/N^2>0$, so every $a_m>0$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:crit}) and every $b_m>0$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:bpos}): the ring is spectrally stable. At $\tau_\infty+d$,
$a_{\lfloor N/2\rfloor}/N^2\le-c_0d+(C_T+C)/N^2<0$ while $b_{\lfloor N/2\rfloor}>0$, so by Prop.~\ref{prop:lin} the ring is
unstable. Hence $|\tau_*(N)-\tau_\infty|\le K/N^2$ for large $N$. Finally
$\cosh r_*=\coth(\tau_*/N)=N/\tau_*+O(1/N)$ and $\sinh r_*=1/\sinh(\tau_*/N)$, so $\ell_N=2\pi/(N\sinh(\tau_*/N))=2\pi/\tau_*+O(N^{-2})$.
\end{proof}
Also $r_*=\ln(2\kappa_\infty N)+o(1)$ and $1-\xi_*\sim2/(\kappa_\infty N)\approx14.89/N$. The second-order expansion
below gives $\ell_N-\ell_*\approx2\pi(C_1-\tfrac1{2\kappa_\infty})/N^2\approx-20/N^2$: finite rings reach stability at a slightly
smaller spacing.

\paragraph{Second order (DERIVED, NUMERICAL check).} Expanding $\Delta_N=\frac1{N^2}\big[\frac{\tau\coth\tau-1}3-\frac12\big]+O(N^{-4})$
and $\varepsilon_N=-\psi_{xx}(0,\tau)/(2N^2)+O(N^{-4})$ (odd $N$) in the threshold equation gives
\[
\cosh r_*=\kappa_\infty N+\frac{C_1}N+O(N^{-3}),\qquad C_1=\frac{\tau_\infty}3-\frac{\mathcal D}{|\Phi'(\tau_\infty)|\,\tau_\infty^2},
\]
with $\mathcal D=\frac{\tau_\infty\coth\tau_\infty-1}3-\frac12$ for even $N$ and $\mathcal D-\frac12\psi_{xx}(0,\tau_\infty)$ for odd $N$.
Numerically $C_1^{\rm even}=0.5412188400$ and $C_1^{\rm odd}=0.4010382403$. The even value explains the coefficient $0.541$ that an
earlier draft obtained by fitting; the odd value is different. Against the certified and the computed thresholds,
$(\cosh r_*-\kappa_\infty N)N$ equals $0.54122060$ at $N=800$ and $0.40103931$ at $N=801$ (\texttt{large\_n.out}). We have
not written out the remainder bounds for this second-order statement.

\paragraph{The energy--momentum threshold.} Positive definiteness of the Hessian on the non-rotational modes requires
$a_mb_m-g_m^2>0$ for every $m$ (Prop.~\ref{prop:lin}). Let $\Phi_N(\tau):=E_N(\tau)-\frac1{4\sinh^2\tau}-\psi(0,\tau)$, so that
$a_m/N^2=\Phi_N+\psi(0,\tau)-\psi(x,\tau)$ and, for even $N$, $\Phi_N=a_{N/2}/N^2$. Put
$R(x,\tau):=\cosh\tau\sinh(x\tau)-x\sinh\tau\cosh(x\tau)$; then $\gamma=R/(4\sinh^2\tau)$ (as in the proof of
Thm.~\ref{thm:symbol}, using $\theta e^{x\tau}-(1-\theta)e^{-x\tau}=\sinh x\tau-x\cosh x\tau$).

\begin{lemma}[PROVED]\label{lem:em}
Let $G(x,\tau):=\psi(0,\tau)-\psi(x,\tau)-\gamma(x,\tau)^2/\beta(x,\tau)$ for $|x|<1$. Then
\[
\beta\,G=\frac{(\cosh\tau+1)^2}{32\sinh^4\tau}\,K(x,\tau),\qquad
K:=\cosh\tau\,(1-\cosh x\tau)+x\sinh\tau\sinh x\tau-\frac{x^2(\cosh\tau-1)}2\big[2-(1-x^2)(\cosh\tau-1)\big],
\]
and $K>0$ for $0<|x|<1$, $\tau>0$. Hence $G\ge0$, with equality only at $x=0$, and for every $N$, $m$ and radius
\[
a_mb_m-g_m^2=N^4\,\beta(x,\tau)\,\big[\Phi_N(\tau)+G(x,\tau)\big].
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Write $C=\cosh\tau$, $S=\sinh^2\tau$. Then $8S(\psi(0)-\psi(x))=A:=x^2S+2C-2Q$, $8S\beta=B:=(1-x^2)S+2-2Q>0$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:bpos}) and $\gamma^2/\beta=R^2/(2SB)$, so $8SB\,G=AB-4R^2$. Expanding gives $Q^2-R^2=C^2-x^2S$, so
$AB-4R^2$ is linear in $Q$; using $S+2C+2=(C+1)^2$ one finds $AB-4R^2=2(C+1)^2K$. For the sign, $K(x,0)=0$ and
\[
\partial_\tau K=\sinh\tau\,(1-x^2)\big[x^2(\cosh\tau-1)-(\cosh x\tau-1)\big]>0\qquad(0<|x|<1,\ \tau>0),
\]
because $\cosh x\tau-1=\sum_{k\ge1}x^{2k}\tau^{2k}/(2k)!<x^2\sum_{k\ge1}\tau^{2k}/(2k)!$. With $\beta>0$ this gives
$G\ge0$. The last identity combines
$a_m/N^2=\Phi_N+\psi(0)-\psi(x)$ with $b_m/N^2=\beta$ and $g_m/N^2=\gamma$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[energy--momentum threshold; PROVED]\label{thm:em}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] For every even $N$ and every radius, the Hessian is positive definite on the non-rotational modes if and only
if $a_{N/2}>0$. Hence for every even $N$ the energy--momentum threshold equals the spectral threshold, and there is no
Krein window. (For $N=2,4,6$ there is no threshold at all: the ring is stable, with definite Hessian, at every radius.)
\item[(b)] For every odd $N\ge9$ the Krein window is non-empty: $\xi_{\rm EM}(N)>\xi_*(N)$; the ring is spectrally
stable on $(\xi_*,1)$, and the critical block is indefinite on a right-neighbourhood of $\xi_*$. Moreover
$\xi_{\rm EM}(N)-\xi_*(N)=O(N^{-3})$.
\item[(c)] For both parities, $\cosh r_{\rm EM}(N)=\kappa_\infty N+O(1/N)$.
\end{enumerate}
Here $\xi_{\rm EM}(N)=\inf\{\xi_0:\text{the Hessian is positive definite on the non-rotational modes for all }\xi\in(\xi_0,1)\}$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(a) $a_m\ge a_{N/2}=N^2\Phi_N$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:crit}). If $a_{N/2}>0$, Lemma~\ref{lem:em} gives $a_mb_m-g_m^2>0$ for every $m$; if
$a_{N/2}\le0$ the Hessian is not positive definite. Spectral stability is $a_{N/2}\ge0$ (Cor.~\ref{cor:alln}). By
Prop.~\ref{prop:evenN}, $a_{N/2}$ has no zero for $N\le6$ and a single simple sign change for $N\ge8$, so the two thresholds
coincide.
(b) For odd $N\ge9$ the planar limit $-(N-1)(N-7)/N^2<0$ of $8a_{\rm crit}/N^2$ and its limit $2-2/N^2>0$ as $r\to\infty$ show
that $\xi_*\in(0,1)$ exists, and $a_{\rm crit}(\xi_*)=0$. There $a_{\rm crit}b_{\rm crit}-g_{\rm crit}^2=-g_{\rm crit}^2<0$,
because $R(1/N,\tau)>0$: $R/(\cosh\tau\cosh x\tau)=\tanh x\tau-x\tanh\tau>0$ by strict concavity of $\tanh$ on
$[0,\infty)$. By continuity the Hessian is indefinite on a neighbourhood of $\xi_*$, so $\xi_{\rm EM}>\xi_*$; by the definition
of $\xi_*$ the ring is spectrally stable on $(\xi_*,1)$. For the width, Lemma~\ref{lem:em} shows the Hessian is positive
definite wherever $\Phi_N>0$ (then also every $a_m>0$). In the proof of Thm.~\ref{thm:largeN},
$\Phi_N=\Phi+\Delta_N$ and $\Phi_N+\varepsilon_N=a_{\rm crit}/N^2$, with $|\Delta_N|,\varepsilon_N=O(N^{-2})$ near $\tau_\infty$. Also
$|\Delta_N'|=O(N^{-2})$: $\delta=\mathcal L(\tau/N)/N$, so $0<\delta'=\mathcal L'(\tau/N)/N^2\le1/(3N^2)$, and differentiating
$\Delta_N=\delta(\coth\tau-\frac1\tau-\frac\delta2)-\frac1{2N^2}$ gives $|\Delta_N'|\le(1+\tau/3)/(3N^2)$. Since $|\Phi'|\ge\frac32c_0$ near
$\tau_\infty$ (shrinking $d_0$ if needed), $\Phi_N'\le-c_0$ there for large $N$. So $\Phi_N$ is positive below its first zero
$\tau_\Phi$ and strictly decreasing near it, and
$\tau_\Phi\le\tau_{\rm EM}\le\tau_*$ with $\tau_*-\tau_\Phi\le\varepsilon_N(\tau_*)/c_0=O(N^{-2})$. Since
$\xi=e^{-2\tau/N}$, $\xi_{\rm EM}-\xi_*\le\frac2N(\tau_*-\tau_\Phi)=O(N^{-3})$.
(c) $\tau_\Phi$ and $\tau_*$ are both $\tau_\infty+O(N^{-2})$, so $\tau_{\rm EM}$ is too, and the conversion is as in
Thm.~\ref{thm:largeN}.
\end{proof}
Part~(a) extends to all even $N$ what Thm.~\ref{thm:cert} certifies for $N\le16$. Part~(b) extends the non-emptiness in
Prop.~\ref{prop:kreincert} (certified for $N=9,11,13,15$) to every odd $N\ge9$. That the critical block stays indefinite on the
whole window, with every other block definite, is certified for $N\le15$ (Prop.~\ref{prop:kreincert}) and checked numerically
for $N\in\{17,21,31,61,121,241\}$; it is not proved in general. The bound in~(b) is crude: the intermediate bound
$\xi_{\rm EM}\le\xi_\Phi:=e^{-2\tau_\Phi/N}$ gives $N^3(\xi_{\rm EM}-\xi_*)\le14.62$ at $N=241$, while the actual value is $0.00348$. Expanding the critical block alone
suggests $N^3(\xi_{\rm EM}-\xi_*)\to W=2\gamma_x(0,\tau_\infty)^2/(\beta(0,\tau_\infty)|\Phi'(\tau_\infty)|)\approx0.0037$; the data
($0.0028$ at $N=61$, $0.0032$ at $N=121$) approach it slowly. That constant is DERIVED, not proved: it assumes that the
critical mode is the last to become definite.

\subsection*{Physical interpretation}
\paragraph{A critical spacing.} Theorem~\ref{thm:largeN} says that a large tanh-model ring becomes spectrally stable once
neighbouring vortices are more than $\ell_*=0.8439\,R$ apart in arc length along the ring (asymptotically), where $R$ is the curvature radius (here
$R=1$). Equivalently, the line density $N/(2\pi\sinh r)$ must be below $1/\ell_*\approx1.185/R$. The approximation
$\eta_\infty\approx2+\sqrt3$ gives $\ell_*\approx\pi(2-\sqrt3)R=0.8418R$. In the sinh model there is no such scale:
$\cosh r_*\sim\sqrt N/2$, so the threshold spacing $\approx\pi/\sqrt N$ tends to $0$ (\S\ref{sec:compare}).

\paragraph{The limit object is a vortex row on a horocycle.} As $N\to\infty$ at fixed $\tau$, the ring radius tends to
infinity while the spacing $2\pi\sinh r/N\to2\pi/\tau$ stays fixed, and the geodesic curvature $\coth r$ of the ring
tends to $1$, that of a horocycle. In the upper half-plane (curvature $-1$, area form $dx\,dy/y^2$) the tanh pair term is
$-\ln\tanh(d/2)=-\ln|z-w|+\ln|z-\bar w|$. Take the horocycle $\operatorname{Im}z=1$ and the infinite row $z_k=k\ell+i$,
$k\in\mathbb Z$. The $x$-translations are isometries with momentum $J=-\sum_j1/y_j$; put $F=H-vJ$. (The pair term and its
image each pull like $1/k$, but together their force decays like $k^{-3}$ in $x$ and $k^{-2}$ in $y$, so the equations of
motion of the row converge absolutely; $H$ and $J$ themselves are infinite and enter only through these.) The Hessian of $F$ at the
row couples sites through absolutely summable terms (each of order $1/k^2$), so on the Bloch wave
$(\delta x_k,\delta y_k)=e^{2\pi i\theta k}(X,Y)$ it acts as a Hermitian $2\times2$ matrix $M(\theta)$, $0\le\theta\le1$.

\begin{proposition}[horocyclic row; PROVED]\label{prop:row}
The row is a relative equilibrium, translating with $v=\frac12(\tau\coth\tau-1)$, where $\tau:=2\pi/\ell$. Its Bloch
symbol is the large-$N$ limit of the ring blocks:
\[
\Big(\frac\ell{2\pi}\Big)^2M(\theta)=\begin{pmatrix}\beta&i\gamma\\-i\gamma&\alpha\end{pmatrix}(x,\tau),\qquad x=1-2\theta,\qquad
\alpha:=E_\infty-\frac1{4\sinh^2\tau}-\psi,
\]
in the basis $(\delta x,\delta y)$. So, after rescaling ($N^{-2}$ for the ring, $(\ell/2\pi)^2$ for the row), the ring's mode
number $m/N$ becomes the Bloch wavenumber $\theta$; the ring's tangential and outward radial directions correspond to $\delta x$
and $-\delta y$, which maps $\theta$ to $1-\theta$ (i.e.\ $\gamma$ to $-\gamma$).
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Write $p_k=1/(k\ell-2i)^2$, so that $p_{-k}=\bar p_k$, and $b=2/\ell=\tau/\pi$. For $h=\operatorname{Re}f$ with $f$ holomorphic,
$\operatorname{Hess}h=\begin{psmallmatrix}\operatorname{Re}f''&-\operatorname{Im}f''\\-\operatorname{Im}f''&-\operatorname{Re}f''\end{psmallmatrix}$.
Applied to $-\ln|z_0-z_k|$ and $\ln|z_0-\bar z_k|$ (in the second, $\partial_{x_k}=-\partial_x$ and $\partial_{y_k}=+\partial_y$), the
on-site block is $\operatorname{diag}(S_1,-S_1)+\sum_{k\ne0}\operatorname{Re}p_k\operatorname{diag}(-1,1)+\operatorname{diag}(0,2v)$, with
$S_1=\sum_{k\ne0}(k\ell)^{-2}$ (the off-diagonal terms cancel in pairs), and the coupling block to site $k$ is
$\operatorname{diag}(-1,1)(k\ell)^{-2}+\begin{psmallmatrix}\operatorname{Re}p_k&\operatorname{Im}p_k\\-\operatorname{Im}p_k&\operatorname{Re}p_k\end{psmallmatrix}$.
Force balance in $y$ gives $v=\sum_{k\ne0}2/(k^2\ell^2+4)$. Four classical sums evaluate everything, for $0\le q:=2\pi\theta\le2\pi$:
\begin{align*}
&\textstyle\sum_{k\ne0}k^{-2}=\pi^2/3,\qquad \sum_{k\ge1}\cos(qk)/k^2=\pi^2/6-\pi q/2+q^2/4,\qquad
\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}(k^2+a^2)^{-1}=(\pi/a)\coth\pi a,\\
&\textstyle\sum_{k\in\mathbb Z}e^{iqk}(k-ib)^{-2}=-2\pi\sum_{n\ge0}(q+2\pi n)\,e^{-b(q+2\pi n)}\quad(b>0).
\end{align*}
The last is Poisson summation, $\sum_kf(k)=\sum_n\hat f(2\pi n)$ with $\hat f(\omega)=\int f(t)e^{-i\omega t}dt$, which applies
because $f(t)=e^{iqt}(t-ib)^{-2}=O(t^{-2})$ and $\hat f$ is continuous and decays exponentially: $\hat f(2\pi n)$ is
$-2\pi(q-2\pi n)e^{-b(q-2\pi n)}$ for $q\ge2\pi n$ (residue at $t=ib$) and $0$ otherwise; the terms with $q=2\pi n$ vanish, so
the formula holds on the closed interval $0\le q\le2\pi$. With $\pi b=\tau$ and
$X=e^{-2\tau}$, the third sum gives $v$, and the fourth gives
$P(\theta):=\sum_{k\ne0}e^{iqk}p_k=\frac14-\tau^2e^{-2\theta\tau}\big[\frac\theta{1-X}+\frac X{(1-X)^2}\big]$. Since
$p_{-k}=\bar p_k$, $P$ is real, $\sum_{k\ne0}\cos(qk)\operatorname{Re}p_k=\frac12(P(\theta)+P(1-\theta))$ and
$\sum_{k\ne0}\sin(qk)\operatorname{Im}p_k=\frac12(P(1-\theta)-P(\theta))$. The bracket in $P$ is the quantity $W_m/N$ of
Thm.~\ref{thm:symbol} (with $\xi^m=e^{-2\theta\tau}$), so by its proof $\frac12(P(\theta)+P(1-\theta))=\frac14-\tau^2Q/(4\sinh^2\tau)$ and
$\frac12(P(1-\theta)-P(\theta))=\tau^2\gamma$. Adding up:
$M_{xx}=\frac{\tau^2}{12}-\frac14+\frac{\tau^2}{4\sinh^2\tau}-\big(\frac{\tau^2}{12}-\frac{\tau^2\theta(1-\theta)}2\big)+\frac14-\frac{\tau^2Q}{4\sinh^2\tau}=\tau^2\beta$,
$M_{yy}=\tau\coth\tau-\frac12-\frac{\tau^2(1-x^2)}8-\frac{\tau^2(1+Q)}{4\sinh^2\tau}=\tau^2\alpha$ and $M_{xy}=i\tau^2\gamma$; finally
$(\ell/2\pi)^2=\tau^{-2}$.
\end{proof}
\texttt{large\_n.out} (item~6) confirms the identity against direct lattice sums to $4\cdot10^{-12}$.

\begin{corollary}[critical spacing of the row; PROVED]\label{cor:row}
The horocyclic row with spacing $\ell$ (in curvature radii) is spectrally stable, i.e.\ every Bloch mode has purely
imaginary exponents, if and only if $\ell\ge\ell_*=\pi/\eta_\infty=0.8439\ldots$. The Bloch matrix $M(\theta)$ is positive
definite for every $0<\theta<1$ if and only if $\ell>\ell_*$; at $\ell=\ell_*$ it degenerates exactly in the pairing mode
$\theta=\frac12$. So the spectral and the definiteness thresholds coincide: there is no Krein window.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
The equations of motion are $\dot q=y^2J_0\nabla F$ in the co-moving frame. Since $dF=0$ at the row, the variation of the
Poisson tensor does not contribute, and the linearisation at $y=1$ is $J_0\operatorname{Hess}F$. On a Bloch wave this is
$J_0M(\theta)$, so as in Prop.~\ref{prop:lin} the exponents are $\sigma=-i\gamma'\pm\sqrt{-M_{xx}M_{yy}}$ with $\gamma'=\tau^2\gamma$
real, and stability means $M_{xx}M_{yy}\ge0$ for every $\theta$. At $\theta=0$, $\beta=\gamma=0$ and
$M(0)=\operatorname{diag}(0,\tau^2\alpha(1,\tau))$ with $\tau^2\alpha(1,\tau)=(\tau\coth\tau-1)+\frac12(1-\tau^2/\sinh^2\tau)>0$: both
exponents vanish, and the null direction is the translation symmetry. For $0<\theta<1$, by
Prop.~\ref{prop:row}, $M_{xx}\propto\beta>0$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:bpos}) and $M_{yy}\propto\alpha=\Phi(\tau)+\psi(0,\tau)-\psi(x,\tau)\ge\Phi(\tau)$,
with equality at $\theta=\frac12$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:crit}). So the row is stable iff $\Phi(2\pi/\ell)\ge0$, i.e.\ iff $\ell\ge\ell_*$.
For definiteness, $\alpha\beta-\gamma^2=\beta(\Phi+G)$ with $G\ge0$, $G=0$ only at $x=0$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:em}).
\end{proof}
Spectral stability here is not bounded linear stability: there is a Jordan block at $\theta=0$ for every $\ell$ (a uniform
change of height makes the row drift), and at $\theta=\frac12$ when $\ell=\ell_*$, so those modes grow linearly. Positive
definiteness also holds only mode by mode: the smallest eigenvalue of $M(\theta)$ tends to $0$ as $\theta\to0$
($\beta=O(\theta^2)$), as for mode $0$ of the ring, and we make no energy--momentum or nonlinear claim for the infinite row.
A direct simulation of a $12$-periodic row (finite differences of the full velocity field, all images summed) reproduces
these exponents to $2\cdot10^{-7}$ and has growth rates $2.60$, $0.98$, $0.47$ at $\ell/\ell_*=0.9$, $0.98$, $0.995$ and none
above $\ell_*$ (independent reader, \texttt{review/horocycle\_row\_review.md}; NUMERICAL).
So Theorem~\ref{thm:largeN} says that a large ring loses stability exactly where the horocyclic row with the same
spacing does. The critical mode $\theta=\frac12$ displaces neighbours in opposite normal directions (its growing
eigenvector also has a tangential part): it is the pairing instability of a vortex row.

\paragraph{Why curvature stabilises.} In (\ref{eq:phi}) the dense limit $\ell\ll R$ ($\eta=\pi/\ell\to\infty$) gives
$8\Phi\to-1$, unstable at every spacing, as for a straight row in the plane (the classical row instability~\cite{Lamb}).
The sparse limit $\ell\gg R$ gives $8\Phi\to2$, stable. Heuristically: the tanh interaction is screened beyond one curvature radius
($-\ln\tanh(d/2)\approx2e^{-d}$), so for large spacing the coupling between neighbours is too weak to drive the pairing
mode, and $\ell_*$ is where the two effects balance.

Both radii grow logarithmically, so $r_*^{\tanh}/r_*^{\sinh}\to2$, but slowly. This is consistent with the data to
$N=800$ (NUMERICAL).

\paragraph{Interpretation.} The models differ by the pair term $\ln\cosh(d/2)$, whose Laplacian is constant. In the sinh
model this background gives a radial stiffness quadratic in $\cosh r$, which beats the Thomson destabilisation $N^2/8$
once $\cosh r\sim\sqrt N/2$. In the tanh model the interaction is screened by curvature: it decays like $e^{-d}$ beyond
one curvature radius. The stiffness is then only linear in $\cosh r$, up to exponentially small image terms, so stabilisation needs
$\cosh r\sim0.134N$.

\paragraph{Flat hard-wall disk} (context only). This model has the Euclidean area form, image vortices, and the
non-constant Robin self-image term; its ring stability goes back to Havelock~\cite{Havelock}, which we have not read.
It is not treated here. The point is only that the same image Green's function, with a different area form and a
non-constant Robin function, is a different stability problem. Neither hyperbolic model describes a flat-bottomed
hard-wall condensate.

\section{Limitations}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Literature.} Boatto~\cite{Boatto} computed the stability ranges of this model numerically, with the same
block structure (\S\ref{sec:motivation}). Her equations reproduce our values exactly; her plotted $K<0$ curves
appear to correspond to a different pair law (\S\ref{sec:motivation}), a reading a specialist should
confirm. Kimura~\cite{Kimura} and
Hwang--Kim 2013~\cite{HK} could not be obtained beyond their abstracts. A human with journal access needs to check
(i)~which Hamiltonian Kimura uses (the tanh attribution here rests on~\cite{MT,MNG,NG,Boatto}) and (ii)~whether
Hwang--Kim 2013 already contains ring stability thresholds for this model. Until then, the novelty claim in
\S\ref{sec:motivation} is provisional.
\item \textbf{No human specialist review.} The derivation has been re-derived and checked by an independent AI reader
(verdict: confirmed with scope fixes, all applied here). No human expert in point-vortex dynamics has reviewed it.
\item \textbf{Large $N$.} The limit $\cosh r_*/N\to\kappa_\infty$ and the critical spacing are proved
(Thm.~\ref{thm:largeN}), and so is the horocyclic-row picture (Prop.~\ref{prop:row}); the $1/N$ coefficients are derived
without remainder bounds. For odd $N\ge17$ uniqueness of the threshold is numerical only.
\item \textbf{Finite range.} Palindromy, irreducibility and the degree pattern are certified only for
$N\le16$ (Conjecture~\ref{conj:deg}). Irreducibility is certified by PARI/GP, not by Lean.
\item \textbf{Nonlinear stability.} This is stated only via Hessian definiteness and modulo $SO(2,1)$ (Remark~\ref{rem:em}). In the
odd-$N$ Krein window the ring is linearly (spectrally) stable and nonlinear stability is undecided; at the threshold
itself it is open.
\end{itemize}
